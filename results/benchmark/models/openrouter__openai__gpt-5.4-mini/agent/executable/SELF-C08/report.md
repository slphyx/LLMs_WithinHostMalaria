I fit a binomial logistic regression to mosquito counts, using the binomial numerator/denominator directly: `mosquitoes_infected ~ Binomial(mosquitoes_examined, p)` with `logit(p) = β0 + βf log10(female gametocytes) + βm log10(male gametocytes)`. This is the simplest defensible model because it respects the experiment-level denominators and uses the sex-specific molecular streams.

Model comparison favored the sex-specific formulation over a single total-gametocyte predictor. Leave-one-out binomial log loss was lower for the sex-specific model (389.35) than for the total-gametocyte model (401.91), so collapsing female and male densities into a total discarded predictive information. Adding asexual density did not help: the sex-specific + asexual candidate had worse leave-one-out log loss (391.54), so asexual density was not retained in the final model. This is an association-only assessment; it does not imply that asexual parasites cause transmission changes.

Prediction uncertainty was quantified with a multivariate normal approximation to the fitted coefficients, propagated to each prediction. For the nine held-out feeds, predicted infection probabilities ranged from near zero to 0.55, and the expected number of infected mosquitoes was 89.5 of 843 examined. Calibration on the training data was close to ideal (calibration intercept -0.10, slope 0.95), with no strong evidence of systematic miscalibration.

Biological interpretation is supported by primary studies: sex-specific gametocyte densities predict mosquito infection better than total gametocyte density alone ([Andolina et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.34463)); Pfs25 and PfMGET provide validated female and male gametocyte qRT-PCR streams ([Schneider et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molbiopara.2015.03.006)); and SBP1 is a sensitive qRT-PCR marker for low-density asexual parasites ([Tadesse et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.4269/ajtmh.16-0893)).
