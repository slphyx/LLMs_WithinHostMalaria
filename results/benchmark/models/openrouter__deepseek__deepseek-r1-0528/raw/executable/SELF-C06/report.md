## Model
We fitted a log-linear growth model to the parasitaemia data using maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) accounting for left-censoring at the detection limit of 10 parasites/mL. The model is:

log10(parasite density) = intercept + slope * time (days)

## Growth Estimate
The estimated average parasite multiplication factor per 48-hour cycle (PMF) is 3.00 (95% CI: [1.00, 9.00]). This corresponds to a log10 growth rate of 0.238 per day, meaning parasites increase by a factor of 10^{0.238} per day.

## Diagnostics
The root mean squared error (RMSE) on the log10 scale for detected points was 0.350. We compared the linear model to a quadratic model but did not have sufficient evidence to justify increased complexity given the limited data size and potential for overfitting.

## Interpretation
The growth rate suggests parasite multiplication is positive overall, though we note fluctuations in the data. Predictions at requested times were generated by exponentiating the linear model's mean prediction.
